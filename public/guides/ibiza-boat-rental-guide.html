Ibiza boat rental guide

Where to go, when to go and what to check before you book a day on the water.

Why see Ibiza by boat

Updated November 2026

Ibiza's best water is hard to reach by road in summer. Car parks at the popular coves fill early, and some of the clearest spots have no road at all. A boat gets you to quiet anchorages before lunch and lets you move on when a cove gets busy.

Most people book a single day, leaving from Ibiza Town, Sant Antoni or Santa Eulària. A day is enough for either the west coast or Formentera, but not comfortably both.

Three routes that work

Formentera and Ses Illetes

The classic day out. Cross from Ibiza Town in roughly 30 to 60 minutes depending on the boat, anchor off the white sand at Ses Illetes and swim at Espalmador.

West coast and Es Vedrà

From Sant Antoni, head south past Cala Bassa and Cala Comte to the rock of Es Vedrà. Stay out for sunset if your booking allows it.

South coast coves

From Ibiza Town, follow the coast to Es Cavallet, Cala Jondal and Porroig. Short hops, calm water on most days and plenty of places to stop for lunch.

When to go

The boating season runs from May to October. July and August are the hottest, busiest and most expensive months, and popular boats are booked well ahead. June and September give you warm water with more space at anchor.

Wind matters more than sunshine. A good skipper picks the sheltered side of the island on the day, so treat any route as a plan rather than a promise.

With a skipper or without

With a skipper you need no licence and no local knowledge, and it is the usual choice for groups. Without one, Spain lets you drive small boats with no licence: up to 5 metres long with a motor of up to 11.26 kW (15 hp), in daylight and close to the coast. Anything bigger needs a recognised boating licence, and the rental company will ask to see it.

What to check before you book

  • Fuel. Ask whether it is included. In Ibiza it is often charged separately at the end of the day.
  • Skipper. Check whether the price includes one and whether they stay with you all day.
  • Hours. A "day" can mean six hours or ten. Confirm the departure and return times.
  • Formentera. Some small boats are not allowed to make the crossing. Ask before you plan around it.
  • Anchoring. Dropping anchor on posidonia seagrass is banned around Ibiza and Formentera and fined. Anchor on sand, or let the skipper handle it.
  • Deposit and cancellation. Know how much is held and what happens if the weather turns.

Questions

Do I need a licence to rent a boat in Ibiza?
Not if you hire a skipper. Without one, Spain allows you to drive small boats up to 5 metres with a motor of up to 11.26 kW (15 hp) without a licence, in daylight and close to the coast. Larger boats need a recognised licence.
Can I get to Formentera and back in a day?
Yes. The crossing from Ibiza Town takes roughly 30 to 60 minutes depending on the boat, so a full-day rental leaves plenty of time at Ses Illetes and Espalmador.
When is the best time to rent a boat in Ibiza?
The season runs from May to October. June and September combine warm water with fewer boats and lower prices than July and August.
