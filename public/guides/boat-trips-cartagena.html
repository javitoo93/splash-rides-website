Boat trips in Cartagena

The islands worth the ride, when to go, and how to choose between a private boat and a shared tour.

Why get on a boat in Cartagena

Updated November 2026

Cartagena's old town is the reason most people come, but the city beaches are not its strong point. The clear water is out at the islands, between 15 minutes and an hour away by speedboat. A day on a boat is how visitors and locals alike get to it.

Where the boats go

Rosario Islands

A national park of coral islands about 45 to 60 minutes from the city. Clear water, snorkelling and lunch at an island restaurant or beach club.

Cholón

A sheltered lagoon on Barú where boats anchor side by side at weekends, with music and vendors selling food and drinks from small boats. The party stop.

Barú and Playa Blanca

Long white-sand beach with turquoise water. Busy by midday, so boats that arrive early get the best of it.

Tierra Bomba

The closest island, 10 to 15 minutes from the city. Good for a half day at a beach club when you do not want a long crossing.

Sunset in the bay

A short cruise around the bay with views of the walled city and the Bocagrande skyline. No open sea, so it suits anyone who gets seasick.

Private boat or shared tour

Shared tours are the cheapest way out to the islands. They leave on a fixed timetable, follow a set route and usually stop at one beach club for lunch.

A private boat costs more in total but is often comparable per person for a group of six or more. You choose the stops and the pace, and you can combine a morning at Cholón with a quieter island for lunch. Most private boats in Cartagena come with a captain, so nobody in the group needs a licence.

When to go

December to April is the dry season and the busiest time on the water. It is also when the trade winds blow, so the sea is usually calmest in the morning and choppier on the ride home. Most boats head back by mid-afternoon for that reason.

September to November is the wettest stretch. Showers tend to be short, prices are lower and the islands are quieter.

What to check before you book

  • What is included. Ask about fuel, captain, ice and snorkel gear. Lunch at the islands is almost always extra.
  • Dock and park fees. Trips to the Rosario Islands usually involve a port fee and a national park fee paid at the dock, on top of the boat price.
  • Departure point. Shared tours tend to leave from the tourist dock by the old town. Private boats usually leave from marinas, so confirm which one.
  • Group size. Every boat has a legal passenger limit that includes children. It is enforced at the dock.
  • Return time. Agree it in advance. The ride back in the afternoon is the bumpiest part of the day.

Questions

How long does it take to get to the Rosario Islands from Cartagena?
About 45 to 60 minutes by speedboat, depending on the boat and the sea.
Do I need a licence to rent a boat in Cartagena?
Not for a boat with a captain, which is how most private boats in Cartagena are offered. The captain drives and your group just enjoys the day.
When is the best time for a boat trip in Cartagena?
December to April is the dry season. Go out in the morning, because the wind picks up in the afternoon and the ride back gets choppier.
